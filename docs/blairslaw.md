# Blair's Law
On July 4, 2011, Blair Shanahan Lane was only 11 years old when she was killed by a stray bullet from celebratory gunfire. 13 years later, on July 9, 2024, Gov. Mike Parson signed a public safety bill into law, which included Blair's Law, establishing state penalties for celebratory gunfire. The law went into effect a month later, on August 28, 2024. The Jackson County Prosecutor's Office welcomed the measure as another tool to prosecute reckless gun use, and hopes enforcement of this law will reduce gun violence and enhance public safety within Jackson County. 

Blair's Law, [RSMo 571.031](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=571.031), now makes it a crime for any individual to recklessly discharge within or into the limits of any municipality within the state. A first offense is a Class A misdemeanor, a second offense is a Class E felony, and third or subsequent offenses are Class D felonies.

    * 571.031-001Y202452 (Class D Felony) if third or subsequent offense
    * 571.031-002Y202452 (Class E Felony) if second offense
    * 571.031-003Y202452 (Class A Misdemeanor) if first offense

# Valentine's Law
Named after St. Louis County detective Antonio Valentine, who was killed in the line of duty after being struck by a fleeing car while inside an unmarked police vehicle in December 2021, Valentine's Law was signed into law by Gov. Mike Parson on July 9, 2024. Valentine's law establishes state penalties for aggravated fleeing a stop or detention of a motor vehicle. The Jackson County Prosecutor's Office has used the new law as another tool to enforce traffic safety and public safety within Jackson County.

Valentine's Law, [RSMo 575.151](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=575.151), creates a new offense, "aggravated fleeing a stop or detention of a motor vehicle," when an individual who knows that a law enforcement officer is attempting to detain the vehicle and the person flees at a high speed which creates a substantial risk of serious physical injury or death or actually causes physical injury or death to another person. 

    * 575.151-001Y202449 (Class A Felony) if the person causes death of another
    * 575.151-002Y202449 (Class B Felony) if the person causes physical injury
    * 575.151-003Y202449 (Class D Felony) if the person creates a substantial risk of injury, without eligibility for probation, parole, or conditional release until the defendant has served no less than one year of such sentence
